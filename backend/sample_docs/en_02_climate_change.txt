Climate Change

Climate change refers to the long-term shift in global temperatures and weather patterns. While Earth's climate has changed naturally throughout its history, the current period of warming is unprecedented and is primarily driven by human activities, especially since the industrial revolution of the 19th century.

The main cause of modern climate change is the burning of fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and natural gas. These activities release greenhouse gases, particularly carbon dioxide and methane, into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat from the sun, creating a greenhouse effect that raises global average temperatures.

The consequences of climate change are widespread and intensifying. Rising sea levels threaten coastal cities, extreme weather events such as hurricanes and droughts are becoming more severe, and ecosystems are being disrupted. Many plant and animal species face extinction as their habitats shift or disappear.

The Paris Agreement, signed in 2015 by nearly every country in the world, aims to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels, with efforts to limit it to 1.5 degrees. Achieving this goal requires a transition to renewable energy, improvements in energy efficiency, and changes in agriculture and transportation.

Individuals can help by reducing energy consumption, using public transport, eating less meat, and supporting policies that address emissions. Addressing climate change is one of the greatest challenges of the 21st century, requiring coordinated global action across nations, industries, and communities.
