The History of the Internet

The Internet began in the 1960s as a United States military project called ARPANET, developed by the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA). The original goal was to create a communication network that could survive partial destruction, such as a nuclear attack. ARPANET first connected computers at four universities in 1969.

In the 1970s, researchers Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn developed the TCP/IP protocol suite, which became the foundational communication language of the Internet. TCP/IP allowed different networks to interconnect, transforming isolated systems into a single global network. On January 1, 1983, ARPANET officially adopted TCP/IP, a date often considered the birth of the modern Internet.

The 1990s brought the World Wide Web, invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN in 1989. The Web made the Internet accessible to non-technical users by introducing hypertext, browsers, and websites. The first graphical browser, Mosaic, was released in 1993 and sparked rapid public adoption.

By the early 2000s, the Internet had become a global utility. Broadband replaced dial-up connections, mobile devices brought Internet access to billions of people, and platforms like Google, Wikipedia, and social networks reshaped how people find information and communicate.

Today, the Internet connects more than five billion people and underpins nearly every aspect of modern life, from education and commerce to healthcare and government. Its history reflects a collaboration between governments, academia, and private industry across more than half a century.
